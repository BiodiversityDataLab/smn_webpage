About us

Our mission

Connecting knowledge, best practices, and data

The Swedish Metabarcoding Network (SMN) aims to improve the technology readiness of metabarcoding as a tool for effective biodiversity monitoring. We are a network of partners across Sweden who collaborate to develop and share best practices, protocols, and data for metabarcoding in Sweden. This covers all parts of the metabarcoding process, from fieldwork via labwork to data analysis and publication.

Part of our mission is to enable other researchers and non-academic partners to utilize metabarcoding and interpret the resulting biodiversity data. There is a particular emphasis on making existing and newly produced datasets available as FAIR biodiversity data, increasing their utility and impact.

What is metabarcoding?

Metabarcoding identifies many organisms at once from the DNA contained in environmental or bulk samples. These seven steps take a sample from the field to FAIR biodiversity data.

  1. 1

    Field sampling

    Collect environmental or bulk samples from terrestrial, freshwater or marine environments.

  2. 2

    DNA extraction

    Extract DNA from samples to capture genetic material from multiple organisms.

  3. 3

    PCR & library prep

    Amplify target markers with PCR and prepare indexed libraries for sequencing.

  4. 4

    Sequencing

    Sequence the libraries using high-throughput sequencing technologies.

  5. 5

    Bioinformatics analysis

    Process sequences, derive ASVs, assign taxonomy and create taxonomy tables.

  6. 6

    ASV portal

    Upload your ASV data to the ASV Portal for curation, annotation, long-term storage and public access in SBDI and GBIF.

    How to upload →
  7. 7

    FAIR biodiversity data

    FAIR data are discoverable, accessible and reusable for research, monitoring and decision-making.

Publishing your data via the ASV portal

Mobilising metabarcoding data to the ASV Portal is one of our core aims. The portal curates and annotates your Amplicon Sequence Variants (ASVs) and makes them publicly available through SBDI and GBIF.

Advantages of uploading your data to the ASV Portal

  1. VisibilityIncrease the visibility and citation of your work. Each dataset receives a permanent GBIF DOI that makes your data citable and traceable in scientific publications.
  2. FAIR DataMake your ASVs Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable. The portal ensures consistent metadata, open formats, and long-term accessibility.
  3. Up to dateYour ASVs are regularly re-annotated using the latest reference databases. This keeps your taxonomic assignments up to date without additional work on your side.
  4. Insight and comparisonCompare your results with other metabarcoding datasets. The portal enables cross-study analyses and helps you identify broader biodiversity patterns.
  5. Contribute to global knowledgeYour uploaded ASV data will be made available on GBIF, supporting biodiversity research and helping build a comprehensive picture of species distributions.
  6. Link to environmental dataView your and other's data in the interactive spatial portal, allowing to link it to a big selection of environmental and contextual layers.

Protocols

Curated protocols for insect metabarcoding workflows, grouped by theme.

Bulk samples to sequencing

  • FAVIS v2: Fast and Versatile protocol for bulk insect metabarcoding

    Non-destructive, high-throughput workflow from lab setup and sample handling to DNA extraction, library prep and sequencing.

    Malaise/bulk insect catches • Cost-effective • Scales to large monitoring projects
  • Sample homogenization and DNA extraction for bulk insect catches

    Whole-catch homogenization and magnetic bead purification protocol to recover additional DNA from bulk trap samples.

    Bulk insect soup workflow • Compatible with FAVIS-derived samples

Standardization and controls

  • Synthetic COI spike-ins for metabarcoding-based insect surveys

    Design, production and storage of synthetic COI spike-ins as internal controls to improve comparability across samples and experiments.

    Internal positive controls • Cross-study comparability • Method harmonization

National biodiversity monitoring programmes

Swedish monitoring programmes that use or are exploring DNA-based methods such as metabarcoding.

Soil · SLU

Swedish Forest Soil Inventory

Long-term monitoring of forest soils at about 2,000 permanent plots across Sweden since 1983. Besides soil chemistry, DNA methods are used to determine fungal community composition.

Visit website
Soil · Upcoming

Agricultural Soil Inventory

An upcoming national inventory of agricultural soils, linked to the EU Soil Monitoring Law (in force since December 2025), which lists loss of soil biodiversity among the key soil threats.

EU Soil Monitoring Law
Freshwater

Freshwater diatom inventory

Diatoms are key indicators of freshwater quality. New DNA barcode reference data for Swedish freshwater diatoms (Kahlert et al. 2026) support metabarcoding-based assessments.

Read the paper
Marine

Invasive species monitoring in harbours

Monitoring of non-indigenous species in Swedish harbours. Contact: Matthias Obst. More information coming soon.

Insects

Swedish Insect Monitoring Scheme (SIMS)

Monitors insects at Swedish monitoring sites using Malaise traps and DNA metabarcoding (running until 2027). Run jointly by NRM, SLU, KTH, Stockholm University and Station Linné.

Visit website
