YOUR FEEDBACK  ·  DATA-PRODUCER SURVEY
What you told us – and what happens next

Thank you to everyone who answered our data-producer survey this spring! 17 research groups took part. Here is what we learned:

  • Lots of data out there: 16 of 17 groups have already produced eDNA/metabarcoding data, mostly deposited as raw reads in ENA or NCBI SRA, some on Dryad, Figshare or Zenodo.
  • Few datasets on the ASV Portal yet: only 3 groups have published data on the ASV Portal so far.
  • Strong willingness to share: 14 of the 16 data-producing groups would like to upload their data to the ASV Portal, and the other two are considering it.
  • Main roadblocks: lack of time; the extra effort of reformatting ASV tables and metadata into the required format; and uncertainty about the process, e.g. who creates the ASV table, or how to handle long-read data and OTU or species-level clusters.
  • Help wanted: 13 of 17 groups would like, or would consider, help from a data steward.

The good news: that help is available now. NBIS offers free data submission support – a data steward guides you through preparing and uploading your data, so it does not have to eat up your time. Want to see how it works first? Join our demo this Friday.

AUSTRALIA & NEW ZEALAND  ·  A MESSAGE FROM SEDNAS
Southern eDNA Society (SeDNAs)

SeDNAs connects researchers, practitioners, government, industry and communities across Australia and New Zealand to advance eDNA science and its practical use. It is a young society, growing from a grassroots network formed in 2019, with its inaugural conference in 2023.

  • Conference: the 3rd Australia & New Zealand eDNA Conference, “Scaling eDNA: From Method to Mainstream”, takes place in Canberra, 16–19 February 2027. Registration is open, early-bird rates until 6 December 2026.
  • Webinars: regular webinars sharing emerging research and practical applications. Next up, on 23 November: a conference preview session.
  • Knowledge Hub: regional best-practice guidelines, technical resources and scientific literature.
  • Responsible practice: a working group supporting ethical partnerships with Indigenous communities and respect for Indigenous knowledge, sovereignty and data governance, plus a High Seas Treaty position statement on marine genetic resource governance.

THE NETHERLANDS  ·  EDNA HELLO
Netherlands eDNA Working Group (NeDNA-WG)
Launch of NeDNA-WG on 6 July 2026 in Leiden

NeDNA-WG was formally launched on 6 July 2026 in Leiden, bringing together around 70 participants from research institutes, government organisations, consultancies, industry and other stakeholders working with environmental DNA. The network aims to accelerate the use of eDNA across sectors by creating a national community for sharing knowledge, experiences and best practices.

Alongside freshwater, marine, soil and airborne eDNA, the network is developing thematic groups on emerging applications in public health and security. Until the first General Assembly in April 2027, NeDNA-WG is guided by an interim chair (Kat Stewart), secretary (Henrik Cornelisson van de Ven) and a six-person steering committee. A website is expected in early 2027, and non-Dutch observational membership may be considered in the future.

CALL FOR ENDORSEMENT
The Milan Statement on Environmental DNA

The Milan Statement was adopted and signed by many participants of the International Workshop on Environmental Genomics (IWEG) in Milan, 7–8 July 2026, and is now open for endorsement by the global eDNA community.

It recognises that eDNA-based tools have reached a level of maturity and reliability that supports their broader use in biodiversity monitoring, management, decision-making and regulation – and calls on industry, natural resource management, environmental policy and regulators to include eDNA methods in formal monitoring toolkits.

If you support the statement, add your name – and feel free to share it with colleagues.
